The world

A haven for the blessed

Some people are born blessed, carrying powers no one taught them. Those powers draw the attention of the calamities, monstrous forces that hunt the blessed wherever they hide.

Valeria is not a kingdom or a region. It is a guild, built as a safe haven where the blessed could live, train and fight together.

History

The guild's story so far

  1. The founding

    The guild is built as a refuge for the blessed, a place the calamities cannot easily reach.

  2. The war

    Every calamity attacks at once. Half the guild's members fall, and so does the old Guild Master.

  3. The disbanding

    With its leader gone and its halls half empty, the guild breaks apart.

  4. The rebuilding · now

    Author, once the guild's receptionist, returns with the old record book to rebuild Valeria better and safer than before. Every new adventurer who signs the book is part of that story.

Who you'll meet

The guild's keepers

Guild Master

Author

Human, in his 30s. Former guild receptionist, now the one holding it all together.

Before the war, Author sat at the front desk of the guild hall. He was the one who signed new members in and kept the guild's great record book, so he knew every name in it and every deed written beside them.

When every calamity struck at once, half of those names were lost, and so was the old Guild Master. The guild broke apart. Author left with the record book, and years later he came back with it, determined to rebuild Valeria better and safer than before.

He is no warrior. He is fragile, and loud sounds still freeze him where he stands. But the book has more in it than names. Its pages are full of spells, and in his hands the guild's own history becomes its weapon.

Tavern Keeper · Retired Mythril

Nova

Snow owl demi-human with ice magic. Farmer and owner of the tavern below the guild hall.

Nova once climbed all the way to Mythril, the highest rank an adventurer can hold. Few in Valeria have ever matched her, and the newest members are nowhere near her strength.

These days she has hung up her adventuring life for a quieter one. She farms her own fields and runs the tavern right below the guild hall, where tired adventurers come for a warm meal, a drink and a seat by the fire.

She is chill and friendly, and she is one of the Guild Master's closest friends. Regulars know two rules of the house: never call her short, and never make her angry. A retired Mythril is still a Mythril.

Tone

What Valeria feels like

A classic medieval fantasy realm: grounded, adventurous, and a little dangerous. This is an MMORPG, not a survival server. Days are spent on combat, dungeons, bosses, farming, roleplay and slice of life.

Between the battles there is quiet too: music in the tavern, harvests to bring in, a hall full of friends.

Feels like
  • Muddy roads and candlelit taverns
  • Stone keeps, deep forests, forgotten ruins
  • Monsters in the dark
  • The Lord of the Rings, The Witcher, Skyrim, D&D, folklore
Isn't
  • Sci-fi or modern
  • Steampunk or cyberpunk
  • Post-apocalyptic
  • Characters dropped in from another world

Character creation

Anyone, as long as they belong here

There is no fixed race list and no power list. Be a human, a dragon-blooded wanderer, a talking cat, an Enderman-touched drifter, or something no one has seen before. Four rules apply to everyone.

Rule 1

Be blessed

Every member of the guild is a blessed individual. Skills alone are not enough: your character must have a power.

Rule 2

Fit the world

Your character should feel born here: castles, villages, kingdoms, wilds and ruins.

Rule 3

No advanced tech

Nothing beyond late-medieval craftsmanship. Check the Technology table on The Guild tab.

Rule 4

Keep it fair

Powerful is fine. Untouchable is not. Every strength needs a cost, a limit or a weakness.

Powers & abilities

Build a power that fights

Powers are combat abilities. Build them around one theme, give each ability a job in a fight, and tie them together with a signature cost.

Before you write your powers

Valeria is an MMORPG server. Combat, dungeons and bosses are a big part of the gameplay loop, alongside farming, roleplay and slice of life. Your Job and Skills cover your daily life, while your Powers are what you bring into a fight.

Valeria is not a generic fantasy survival server. Things like breathing underwater, going without food or resisting poison won't come up much here.

Answer 1

What does it do?

"I raise walls of ice and throw frozen feathers."

Answer 2

Where does it come from?

Magic, a curse, a bloodline, a god, a relic, a pact or training.

Answer 3

What does it cost?

Stamina, pain, time, materials or a side effect.

Answer 4

When does it fail?

In sunlight, near iron, when tired, or after one use a day.

Give each ability a job

Damage

Hits hard: projectiles, slams, bursts

Tank

Takes hits: shields, armor, walls

Support

Helps allies: heals, buffs, cleanses

Control

Moves enemies: pulls, slows, roots

Mobility

Moves you: dashes, leaps, blinks

Body traits go elsewhere. Everyday passives like poison resistance, no fall damage or eating blocks belong under Race or Skills. Combat passives like regeneration or extra armor still count as Powers.

Your magic can be anything. You are not limited to fire, ice or lightning. Puppets on glowing strings, ink creatures painted in the air, a bell that sends out a shockwave, or clouds of spores all work. Weird and specific powers are often the most memorable ones.

The theme check
Fits the theme
  • Puppets on strings and carved dolls
  • A cursed blade that grows stronger with blood
  • Fire from a pact with a flame spirit
  • Calling down a storm on a battlefield
  • Shapeshifting into a raven
Out of theme
  • Robots, mechs or sci-fi tech
  • Guns, phones, cars
  • Powers copied from another series
  • An all-powerful god who can't be beaten
  • Joke or meme powers
It has to work in-game

If you can see it, hit with it, or feel its effect, it can be built. If it only happens inside someone's head or in the story, it can't.

Can be built
  • Raising stone walls, tearing open the ground
  • A swarm of crows that dives at enemies
  • Turning into a wolf, bear or raven
  • Craters, meteor strikes, big flashy spells
Can't be built
  • Reading minds or thoughts
  • Making another character obey, fear or love you
  • Seeing the future or rewinding time
  • Powers that only exist in your backstory
Sources of power

Every guild member is born blessed. The source is how that blessing shows itself, or what it is bound to. Training and skill sharpen a power, but they can't replace one.

Naming

A name that belongs here

  • Keep it pronounceable. Aldric, Mira, Thorne and Elowen work. Xz'rqqlith is hard for everyone.
  • Add a family name or title. Mira of Greywater, Thorne Ironhand, Old Bess the Baker.
  • Draw on real languages. Old English, Norse, Celtic, Latin, Slavic, Japanese and Korean roots all work.
  • No modern or joke names. No Kevin, no xX_Slayer_Xx, no famous characters from other media.
  • Your username doesn't have to match your character's name.
Roleplay

Rules of the table

These keep roleplay fun for everyone, not only the strongest character.

  • No godmodding. You can't be unbeatable, dodge everything, or decide what another character does or feels.
  • No metagaming. Your character only knows what they learned in-character.
  • No powergaming. Don't invent new powers mid-scene to win a fight.
  • Respect consent. Major harm, permanent injury or death needs both players to agree.
  • Keep IC and OOC separate. What a character does isn't what the player does.
  • Stay in the setting. Slang, memes and real-world references go in out-of-character chat.

Character death

Permanent death only if you agree, or in a staff event clearly marked as deadly. Losing a fight means injury, capture or retreat.

Character growth

Growth is earned in roleplay, not added on paper. Losses matter too: a broken weapon or a scar can make a stronger story than another upgrade.

Approval

Adventurer registration

  1. Fill in the character sheet.
  2. Post it in the adventurer registration channel, or send it to a staff member.
  3. Staff approve it, or ask you to change things like a power that's too strong or tech that doesn't fit.
  4. Once approved, your character is ready to play.

Small edits like appearance or backstory details are fine anytime. New powers, a new race or a higher tier need staff approval again. You can have more than one character, each with its own approved sheet.

Questions

FAQ

Can my character be a Minecraft mob, like an Enderman or a Creeper?

Yes. Describe how they live in this world and what their limits are.

Can I be an immortal or a god?

You can play a long-lived race like an elf or a spirit. True immortality and godhood are reserved for staff lore.

Does my magic have to be an element?

No. Puppet, ink, bell, bone, or something completely new are all welcome.

Do I have to have powers?

Yes. Everyone in the guild is a blessed individual, so every character needs a power. Skills like swordplay or tracking go on top of it, never instead of it.

Can I use redstone in my story?

Yes, but treat it as a strange kind of magic or ancient craft, not electricity.

Can I have a pet or companion?

Yes. Animals, familiars and summoned creatures are fine. Add them under Weapons & Gear or Powers.

Can my character know another player's character?

Yes, but agree on it with that player first.

The guild's old record book is the levelling system. When a character takes part in a lore event, their deed is inscribed. Each inscription is worth Ink, and Ink is what deepens a blessing.

Players can open their page in-game at any time. It works as a level counter and a trophy shelf for roleplay.

Chapter IV

Power tiers

These are the same five tiers as the character creation guide. New characters start at Novice or Adept, and Ink carries them upward. What each tier unlocks is written per character, so every power still feels its own.

A tier is a big moment, not a routine reward. Reaching one takes several seasons of taking part in the story.

1

0 Ink
2

Signature move, such as a rage or ultimate

100 Ink + Calamity · season 5+
Rule

20 Ink per season, at most

Whatever you take part in, a season can add no more than 20 Ink to your Chronicle. The tiers above are the fastest anyone can possibly climb.

Rule

One tier per season

Even with Ink banked from earlier seasons, a character can rise only one tier in a season. New tiers are granted at the end of a season, all at once.

Example · Nova

As a Novice she has Ice Feathers and Grab. Dive and Defend arrives at Adept, Ice Tornado at Master, and Rage Mode only once she becomes a Legend. That is why a retired Mythril adventurer outclasses anyone fresh off the road.

Legend is the rarest tier on the server. It can only be reached by standing against a Calamity and surviving it.

Chapter VII

Keeping it fair

Echoes

Past Sagas can be replayed as instanced Echoes for half the Ink, so players in other time zones can catch up.

Witness credit

Hearing a tale from someone who was there gives 1 Ink once per event. It rewards roleplay and costs staff nothing.

First time counts

Full Ink only on your first clear. Repeats give the story, not the power.

Season cap

No more than 20 Ink per season. Veterans can't race ahead, and newer players close the gap season by season.
